# Valentine's Scavenger Hunt Planning Guide
## From "I Have an Idea" to a Night They'll Never Forget

A great scavenger hunt doesn't happen the day before Valentine's Day. It happens because someone took 20 minutes two weeks out to plan it properly. This guide walks you through exactly what to do and when — so the experience is seamless, romantic, and genuinely feels magical rather than stressful.

---

## Your Planning Timeline

### 2 Weeks Before Valentine's Day

**Choose your hunt format.**
Decide between the indoor romantic dinner hunt and the outdoor adventure hunt before doing anything else. The choice should be based on:
- Your partner's personality (do they love adventure and surprise, or prefer cozy and intimate?)
- Practical logistics (weather, location access, your cooking confidence)
- What they would least expect

You can also combine them: a short outdoor opener (3–4 clues) that leads back home to an indoor finale.

**Read through your chosen clue set completely.**
Go through every clue and ask: does this location exist in my home or in our relevant outdoor spaces? If a clue mentions "where we keep the extra blankets" and you don't have a linen closet, you'll need to substitute. Mark any clues that need to be adapted to your specific space.

**Note any shopping you'll need to do.**
Most clues suggest small romantic additions — candles, flower petals, a small gift at the final location. Make a master list now while you have time.

---

### 1 Week Before

**Personalize your clues.**
Go through each clue and add one personal detail that makes it yours. A clue that says "the place where you warm up every morning" becomes even more special if you add a line that references something specific — "where you always text me before you're fully awake." The generic versions work. The personalized versions are the ones they'll talk about for years.

**Write your love notes.**
Select 6–8 love notes from the love notes collection to attach to clue locations throughout the hunt. Some should be funny, some heartfelt. Vary the tone — emotional whiplash between laughter and genuine feeling is part of what makes this memorable.

**Gather your supplies.**
Standard supply list for the indoor hunt:
- 10 envelopes or small cards for the clues
- Decorative string or ribbon to seal them (optional but nice)
- At least 3–4 candles
- Rose petals or another decorative element for the final reveal
- Their favorite small gift or treat for the final clue
- Ingredients for the dinner reveal (if cooking)

For the outdoor hunt, add:
- A way to keep clues weatherproof (small zip-lock bags work perfectly)
- Backup tape or pins to secure clues at each location

**Plan the final location.**
The final clue is the most important one. It should lead to something genuinely special — not just "the next clue." The indoor hunt leads to a set dinner table. The outdoor hunt should lead to somewhere meaningful: the coffee shop where you had your first date, a park with a significant memory, or back home to something you've set up. Plan this final moment first, then work backwards.

---

### 3 Days Before

**Write and print your clues.**
Use the clue templates exactly as written, or adapt them using the customization tips in each clue entry. Print them on cardstock if possible — it makes a difference in how the whole experience feels versus printing on regular paper.

**Confirm your dinner reservation or grocery list.**
If the hunt ends at a restaurant: confirm the reservation and make sure the restaurant knows it's Valentine's Day (some have special menus or can add something to the table ahead of time).

If cooking at home: buy your non-perishable ingredients now so the day-of shopping is minimal.

---

### Day Before Valentine's Day

**Set up everything you can.**
Hide all the clues that are in locations that won't be disturbed overnight. In a home with a partner who won't be snooping, you can set up most of the indoor hunt the night before. Do not leave the final location set up for more than a few hours — candles should be lit right before they arrive at the finale, not the night before.

**Prepare the first clue delivery method.**
The first clue is the one that starts the whole experience. Think about how you want to deliver it:
- Handed to them with a handwritten note explaining the hunt
- Hidden in an obvious "first spot" with a note that says "look here first"
- Attached to a Valentine's Day card you give them as usual — with the scavenger hunt as the surprise inside

**Confirm your backup plan.**
If you're running an outdoor hunt and it rains: which clues can be moved indoors? Have two alternate locations ready for each outdoor stop just in case. The planning guide has backup options for each outdoor clue in the adventure hunt.

---

### Valentine's Day

**Give yourself 30 minutes before they arrive (or wake up).**
Do a walk-through. Check that every clue is in the right place, undisturbed, and legible. Light any candles you can safely leave unattended. Set the final location.

**Deliver the first clue and stay out of the way.**
Once the hunt starts, let it run. Don't follow them from clue to clue — let them have the experience of discovery. Make yourself busy elsewhere so they're not self-conscious. Be at the final location when they arrive.

**Have a camera ready at the finale.**
This is genuinely the moment you want a photo. Not the whole hunt — the final reveal.

## Customization Tips

**Making clues personal:**
Every clue in this kit is written to work for any couple. To make them exceptional, replace any generic reference with something specific to your relationship. "Where we relax together" becomes "where you always steal the left side of the couch." Small specifics communicate: I pay attention. I know you.

**Difficulty calibration:**
The clues are written at a medium difficulty — clear enough to solve without frustration, specific enough that they're not obvious on first read. If your partner is very analytical and loves puzzles, add a second layer to each clue (a short riddle before the location hint). If they're more about the romance than the puzzle, make each clue more direct and let the love notes carry the emotional weight.

**Adding activities between clues:**
Several clues in both hunts have optional "romantic activity" suggestions. These are moments where you've set up something small at the clue location — a note that says "dance with me for one song before opening the next clue," or a playlist queued up, or their favorite snack waiting for them. These pauses slow the hunt down and make it feel like an experience rather than a race to the finish. Use at least 2–3 of them.

**Involving others:**
If you want to include friends or family in the experience (particularly for the outdoor hunt where logistics are more complex), they can help with: watching a location to make sure a clue doesn't blow away, being at a "checkpoint" with a small gift, or filming a secret video message on your phone left at one of the clue locations.

## Backup Plans

**If they find a clue early:**
Don't panic. Just redirect: "That's a real clue — but you're not supposed to be there yet. Go back to [first location] and start from the beginning."

**If a location isn't accessible:**
Outdoor hunts are vulnerable to closures, bad weather, or changes. If a location on your planned route is unavailable, pivot to the alternate location from your backup list and tape the clue there instead. Keep a text document on your phone with alternate location options for each stop.

**If the dinner isn't ready when the hunt ends:**
Have a first course ready — even a simple charcuterie board or champagne — so there's something to present at the final reveal even if the main meal needs another 30 minutes. The reveal moment matters more than food timing.

**If they don't want to play:**
Some people — even people who love romantic gestures — feel awkward being surprised with an activity. If you know your partner is in this category: give them a heads-up that you've planned something special tonight and let them know it'll be fun. The element of surprise in a scavenger hunt comes from the clues and locations, not from not knowing it's happening at all.

## Shopping List

**For the Indoor Romantic Dinner Hunt:**
- 10 envelopes or card sets
- Candles (minimum 4, ideally more for the finale)
- Rose petals or flower petals for the final reveal
- Their favorite chocolate or small treat for the final clue
- Ingredients for the dinner you're serving at the reveal
- A small wrapped gift (optional but elevates the finale significantly)
- 1 bottle of wine, champagne, or their preferred drink

**For the Outdoor Adventure Hunt:**
- 10 small zip-lock bags (to weatherproof clue cards)
- Tape or pin stakes for outdoor clue mounting
- A charged portable speaker (optional, for a playlist moment at the final location)
- Transportation/parking plan for all locations
- Final destination setup supplies (depends on your ending — picnic blanket, restaurant reservation card, a photo album or gift)
- Comfortable shoes (for you — you're setting up before they start)
